01 · Introduction

Project Overview

This project presents an AI-powered system designed to assess the freshness and quality of fruits and vegetables using state-of-the-art computer vision and zero-shot learning techniques. Rather than relying on traditional machine learning approaches that require large labelled datasets, this system leverages OpenCLIP — a contrastive language-image pretraining model — to achieve accurate classification across dozens of produce categories without any domain-specific training data.

The system integrates three core analytical modules: zero-shot classification, surface defect detection, and freshness estimation — all delivered through a clean, accessible web interface built with Flask and modern CSS.

02 · Context

Why Food Quality Matters

🌍

Food Security

With a global population exceeding 8 billion, reliable access to nutritious food is a critical challenge. Accurate freshness assessment reduces unnecessary disposal and stretches food supply further.

💰

Economic Impact

Food spoilage costs the global economy over $1 trillion annually. Retailers, distributors, and consumers all suffer losses from produce that spoils before consumption.

🏥

Public Health

Consuming spoiled produce can cause foodborne illnesses. An automated quality system provides an additional safety layer, especially in regions with limited food inspection infrastructure.

🌱

Environmental Benefit

Food waste accounts for 8–10% of global greenhouse gas emissions. Reducing waste through intelligent inspection directly supports climate and sustainability goals.

03 · Theory

What is Computer Vision?

Computer Vision (CV) is a field of artificial intelligence that trains computers to interpret and understand the visual world. Using digital images and videos, CV systems can accurately identify objects, classify scenes, detect patterns, and make decisions — mimicking human visual cognition at machine speed and scale.

In the context of this project, computer vision is applied to two primary tasks:

📸

Image Classification

Identifying which fruit or vegetable appears in the image, with a confidence score for each candidate category.

🔎

Defect Segmentation

Identifying pixels that represent unhealthy surface conditions using color space analysis, quantifying the defect as a percentage of total area.

04 · Foundations

Deep Learning Basics

Deep learning is a subset of machine learning that uses artificial neural networks with many layers (hence "deep") to learn representations of data. Unlike traditional hand-crafted feature engineering, deep networks automatically discover the features most useful for a given task through training on large datasets.

1

Input Layer

Raw pixel values from the image are fed into the network. For CLIP, images are divided into patches (like ViT architecture).

2

Feature Extraction (Encoder)

Each transformer layer learns increasingly abstract representations — from edges and textures to semantic concepts like "ripe tomato" or "mouldy surface".

3

Embedding Space

The image is projected into a high-dimensional vector space where similar images cluster together. This embedding is what CLIP compares against text embeddings.

4

Similarity Scoring

Cosine similarity between image and text embeddings produces a confidence score for each candidate label. The highest scorer is the final classification.

05 · Key Innovation

Zero-Shot Learning

Zero-shot learning (ZSL) enables a model to classify objects it has never explicitly seen during training. Instead of requiring labelled examples of every class, ZSL models are trained to understand the semantic relationship between visual features and descriptive text.

✅

Advantages of ZSL

No domain-specific dataset collection. Works on new produce categories immediately. Reduces annotation cost and labelling effort dramatically.

⚡

How it works here

We define text prompts like "a fresh red apple" for 50+ produce types. CLIP compares the uploaded image to all prompts simultaneously and returns the best match.

06 · Architecture

Multimodal AI

Multimodal AI refers to models that can process and relate information from multiple types of input — in CLIP's case, images and text. Both modalities are encoded into a shared embedding space, allowing direct comparison between a visual scene and a textual description.

🔗

Contrastive Pretraining

CLIP was trained on 400 million image-text pairs from the internet. During training, it learns to push matching image-text pairs closer together in embedding space and pull non-matching pairs apart. This contrastive objective gives it a rich, generalisable understanding of visual semantics.

09 · Scoring

Freshness Estimation Formula

The freshness score is derived from a weighted combination of the CLIP confidence score and the defect score, ensuring both visual classification quality and surface condition contribute meaningfully:

Freshness (%) = (Confidence × 0.6) + ((100 − Defect) × 0.4)
✅

Fresh

Freshness ≥ 72% and defect < 30%

⚠️

Caution

Freshness 40–72% or defect 30–60%

❌

Non-Consumable

Freshness < 40% or defect > 60%
